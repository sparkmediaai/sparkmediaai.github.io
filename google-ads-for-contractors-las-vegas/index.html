Google Ads for contractors in Las Vegas

A homeowner with no cooling, a leaking roof, or a garage door that will not open searches, then calls. Here is how those ads work for Las Vegas contractors: the campaign type, the cities, and the follow-up after a missed call.

Before the campaign

Decide the job before you pick the campaign.

Start from the work you want on the schedule. Emergency calls, replacement estimates, and small service tickets need their own searches, their own ads, and often their own map.

On paid media work, the ad is tied to the page, the call, and the follow-up. The sections below are the decisions Las Vegas contractors usually settle before any of that is turned on.

Two products

Search ads and Local Services Ads do different jobs.

Search ads are the campaigns you build in Google Ads. You choose the searches, such as AC repair or a water heater. You write the ad. You send the click to a phone call or to a page. Google charges when someone clicks.

Local Services Ads are a separate product. For categories Google currently offers, a business profile can show on local searches, and the charge is for a lead, usually a phone call or a message. You set the categories, the service area, and the budget. Keyword control sits with the Search campaign, where you pick the searches yourself.

Check eligibility in the Google account. A trade that is open in another city can be closed here, and the list moves. When you need control over the job and the city, use Search ads. When Local Services is available and the leads match work you will take, add them. Some shops run both. Someone still has to answer.

Service area

Limit the map to cities you will actually drive.

Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, and Summerlin are separate drives. Afternoon traffic turns a pin on a map into a long trip. A radius around the shop also pulls in places you may never work, including parts of Enterprise and Spring Valley.

Set the cities and ZIP codes you will accept, and exclude the rest. If Henderson is the whole territory, North Las Vegas searches stay out. If emergency calls can travel farther than estimates, give those campaigns different areas.

Name the cities in the ad. A Henderson homeowner who only sees a generic Las Vegas line cannot tell whether a truck is coming. If you will turn down a small job on the other side of the valley, keep that invitation out of the ad.

After the first weeks, read where the calls came from. If a city you do not serve is still in the report, the targeting is wrong, even when the dispatcher is already declining those jobs.

Calls

Track the call, and answer it.

Use a tracking number on the ad or the landing page so you can see which calls came from Google. Forward that number to the phone the office already picks up. A second phone that lives in a drawer will create missed calls.

Run the ads during hours a person answers. If the office is closed and nobody takes after-hours emergencies, pause the ads outside those hours. A nighttime call that reaches voicemail is a missed job, and on Search ads it is still a click.

Missed calls need a same-day follow-up. Call the person back. If you text, keep the message about that missed call, and send it only through a process that already has the consent your texts require. The message is there to recover the job.

Each week, review a sample of the calls. Note the job, the city, and whether you booked it. A long call log is a weak signal when the calls are for work you do not do.

Budget

Set the budget from the work you can take.

Keep two costs apart. Ad spend goes to Google. A management fee, if you pay one, goes to the person running the account. Folding them into a single marketing total makes it hard to see which one to change.

Google paces ad spend across the month. Raising it gives the ads more chances to show. The searches, the ad, and the person answering still decide whether those chances become booked jobs.

Tie the amount to open capacity. A full crew is a reason to spend less until you can take the work. A week with room for emergency calls is a reason to stay visible on those searches. At the end of the month, put the ad spend next to the jobs you wanted and booked, then raise, hold, or cut it on purpose.

Skip budget worksheets written for a different trade or a different climate. A roof replacement and a lockout support different spend, and a Las Vegas summer is its own season.

Seasonality

Match the ads to the Las Vegas year.

Summer heat changes HVAC demand. More people search for AC repair and no-cool help while the temperature stays high. Cover the phones before that stretch, and compare those weeks with other hot weeks.

Storms change roofing demand. Leak searches pick up after rain, then the mix moves back toward replacement and inspections. Have the leak ads ready. If the crew is booked on larger jobs during a dry stretch, narrow those ads.

Plumbing, electrical, locksmith, and garage door emergencies run all year. People search when something fails. Keep those campaigns on the emergency terms you want.

Tree removal and other outdoor work are harder to schedule in extreme heat. In those months, advertise work you can still book, and shrink the map if the drive plus the heat means you will decline the job.

Measurement

What to look at each week.

Clicks show that someone chose the ad. This list shows whether the spend should stay.

  • Calls and form leads from the ads
  • The search that triggered each one
  • City and job type
  • Answered, missed, and whether anyone followed up
  • Booked or declined
  • Search terms to pause, including DIY, hiring, parts only, and cities outside the area
  • Ad spend next to the jobs that booked

Timing

Judge it after a few consistent weeks.

Once Google approves the ads, impressions can start, and calls follow when people are searching. The first days include odd queries and calls that were never going to book. Pause the searches you do not want as they show up.

Review the account after a few consistent weeks, with the phone answered during ad hours. Bring the search terms, the missed calls, and the jobs that booked. Change the keywords, the area, or the budget from that list.

There is no calendar day when a campaign flips from learning to working. If you want a second look, book a strategy call or contact Spark Media. Bring the cities you serve and the jobs you want.

Questions

Questions Las Vegas contractors ask.

Should a Las Vegas contractor use Google Search ads or Local Services Ads?

Search ads are campaigns you build in Google Ads. You choose the searches, write the ad, and send the person to a call or a page. You pay Google when they click. Local Services Ads are a separate product. For categories Google currently offers, a business profile can show on local searches, and you pay for a lead, usually a call or a message. Google decides which categories and businesses qualify, and that list changes, so check the account instead of assuming your trade is included in Las Vegas. Use Search ads when you need to limit the campaign to specific jobs and cities. Add Local Services Ads when your category is eligible and the leads are jobs you will take. Some shops run both. Either way, someone has to answer the phone.

How should I target Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, and Summerlin?

Target the places you will send a truck. Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, and Summerlin are different drives, and a radius around the shop often includes neighborhoods you do not serve, such as parts of Enterprise or Spring Valley. Choose the cities and ZIP codes you want, and exclude the rest. If you only work in Henderson, exclude North Las Vegas searches. Put the cities you serve in the ad so people know a truck will come to them. An emergency call across the valley is a different decision from an estimate you only want near the shop, so set the area for the job.

How do call tracking and missed-call follow-up work?

Call tracking puts a number on the ad, or on the landing page, so you can see which calls came from the ads. Forward that number to the phone your office already answers. The record shows the search, the time, and whether someone picked up. Missed calls need a same-day next step. Call the person back. If you text, keep the message about that missed call, and send it only through a process that already has the consent your texts require. Review the calls often enough to separate a real job from a wrong number, an out-of-area request, or a search you do not want.

How should a contractor set a Google Ads budget?

Keep two costs apart. Ad spend is what you pay Google to show the ads. A management fee, if you pay one, is what you pay the person running the account. Google spreads the ad spend across the month. Raising it gives the ads more chances to show. The searches, the ad, and the person who answers still decide whether those chances become jobs. Set the spend from open capacity. A full schedule is a reason to spend less until you can take the work. A week with room for emergency calls is a reason to stay visible on those searches. At the end of the month, put the ad spend next to the jobs you wanted and booked, then raise, hold, or cut it on purpose. A figure copied from another city or another trade is a poor guide.

Does seasonality change Google Ads for Las Vegas contractors?

Yes. Searches follow the weather and the work. In summer, HVAC companies see more AC repair and no-cool searches while the heat holds. Roofers see more leak searches after storms. Plumbing, electrical, locksmith, and garage door emergencies show up all year. Tree work and other outdoor jobs are harder to schedule in extreme heat, so non-urgent ads may need a smaller area or a clearer offer in those months. Compare a slow month with another slow month. Staff the phones for the rush you already know is coming. Ads during a heat wave only help if someone can answer.

What should a contractor track besides clicks?

Track the calls and form leads from the ads, the searches that produced them, and whether the job was in your service area. Note missed calls and how quickly someone followed up. Track booked jobs along with the rings. Read the search terms report and pause queries you do not want, such as DIY, hiring, parts only, or cities you do not serve. Put ad spend next to the jobs that booked. Clicks show that the ads were chosen. Booked work shows whether they were worth running.

How long until Google Ads results are useful?

You can see impressions and calls after Google approves the ads and they start running. The first days are noisy: odd searches, some wrong calls, and too few booked jobs to judge the account. Keep it running long enough to read the search terms, cut wasted spend, and see which calls become work you wanted. Review it after a few consistent weeks of ads and answered phones, then change what the list tells you to change. There is no set day when a campaign starts working. Extra time does not fix a phone nobody answers.

Do I need a new website before running Google Ads?

Not for every job. Emergency trades can use call-only ads, where the ad itself is the call button. Planned work, such as a roof replacement or a tree estimate, usually needs a page that states the job, the cities you serve, and how to reach you. Use the path that matches how that customer hires. If the page is slow, confusing, or silent about the service area, fix that before you send more clicks to it.
