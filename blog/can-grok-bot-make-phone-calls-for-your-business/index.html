Can Grok Bot Make Phone Calls for Your Business?

November 5, 2026 · By David McCormick · 6 min read

Yes. Grok Bot can now place real phone calls to US businesses for you: it dials, works through the phone menu, waits on hold, talks to the person, and sends you a transcript and summary. For a business, it fits best on calls to vendors, suppliers, insurers, and offices, where you need an answer but don't need to be on the line. It is not a sales dialer: the terms say you may only call people who have agreed to hear from you, and marketing or mass calls aren't allowed.

What can Grok Bot do on a phone call?

It can call a person or business, make a request for you, take notes on the answer, and report back. That's how @elonmusk has described it, and the official @bot account announced this week that calls are live.

Before it dials, the bot shows a phone call card with who it plans to call, and nothing happens until you approve it. During the call you can follow a live transcript, and if the other side asks for something the bot doesn't know, such as an account PIN or a one-time code, it asks you in the chat and relays your answer. When the call ends you get a summary and the full transcript.

Which business calls are a good fit?

The calls that eat your time but don't need your judgment in the moment. Good examples for a small business:

  • Vendor and supplier questions: checking stock, lead times, or the status of an order.
  • Insurance and billing: asking what a policy covers, why a charge changed, or what a renewal will cost.
  • Offices and agencies: confirming hours, required documents, or the status of a permit or application.
  • Service providers: scheduling a repair, rebooking a delivery window, or reporting an outage.
  • Research before a purchase: getting the details a company's website leaves out.

Builders are already shaping bots around this. @BrianRoemmele shared a setup he calls a phone secretary for the calls people dread, like banks, insurance companies, utilities, and doctor's offices.

What are the rules for business calls?

You are the caller of record, so you're responsible for following calling laws, including the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. Section 2.7 of the Grok Bot Terms spells out the main points:

  • You may only call people who have agreed to hear from you, and you must get any required consent.
  • No marketing or mass calls, and no fraud, harassment, impersonation, or emergency calls.
  • Every call says it's a virtual assistant calling on your behalf and may be recorded, and you can't turn that off.
  • Calls may come from temporary numbers, so it isn't a replacement for your business phone line.

In practice, that means calls to vendors and offices are a natural fit, while cold calling leads is off the table. For following up with your own customers, text and email through your CRM, sent with their consent, is still the right channel.

How do you get a good result from a call?

Give the bot a clear goal, the facts it will need, and the limits it must stay inside. A good request names who to call, why, what a successful outcome looks like, and what it should not agree to.

  1. State the outcome. "Find out if the March 12 delivery can move to March 14" works better than "call the supplier."
  2. Share the details up front. Account numbers, order numbers, and dates save time on hold and reduce questions mid-call.
  3. Set limits. Tell it not to agree to new charges, contracts, or upgrades without asking you first.
  4. Stay reachable. Keep the chat nearby in case the business needs a code or a quick decision.
  5. Read the transcript. It shows what was actually agreed, not just that the call connected.

Expect some calls to need you anyway. Early users report that a few companies won't make account changes once they know an AI is on the line.

What limits should you know about?

For now, calling is US only, and there are caps on how many calls and how long. One independent write-up reports limits it saw in the call tool as of November 3: no calls for users or numbers in Washington or Illinois, a longest call of 85 minutes, and up to 10 calls a day with 4 to the same number. Those can change, so check what your own bot reports.

Usage is lighter than many expected. @Texas_Twins_Dad posted that an 85-second call used 0.2% of his weekly usage, with no separate charge.

Frequently asked questions

Can Grok Bot make phone calls?

Yes. Grok Bot can call US businesses and people for you after you approve a call card, then send you a live transcript and a summary of what was said.

Can I use Grok Bot to call leads or run sales calls?

No. The Grok Bot Terms prohibit marketing and mass calls and say you may only call people who have agreed to hear from you. Use consent-based text and email for customer follow-up.

Does the person on the call know it's an AI?

Yes. Every call identifies itself as a virtual assistant calling on your behalf and notes that it may be recorded, and that disclosure can't be turned off.

What happens if the business asks for a PIN or code?

The bot asks you in the chat during the call, and relays your answer to the person on the line. Stay near your phone or computer for calls that need account checks.

Can Grok Bot answer my business phone?

Calls are built for the bot to place calls on your behalf and may use temporary numbers, so they don't replace your business line or a dedicated answering setup.
