Should You Let Grok Bot Join Your Business Meetings?

November 13, 2026 · By David McCormick · 6 min read

Yes, for the right meetings. Grok Bot can now join a Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams call from a link, answer questions in the meeting chat, and send a summary afterward. That is useful for internal check-ins, project calls, and sales follow-up, as long as everyone on the call knows the Bot is there and you decide in advance what it is allowed to do with what it hears.

What can Grok Bot do in a meeting now?

Join from a link, follow the conversation, answer in the chat, and send a recap when the call ends. The Grok Bot changelog entry for November 11 says that on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans a Bot can join a Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams call from a link, answer in the meeting chat, and send a summary afterward. The Grok Bot 1.0 announcement adds that Bots can bring information from earlier conversations into the discussion and keep track of decisions.

People testing it on X describe the same flow. @alexmichael wrote that you can paste a Meet, Zoom, or Teams link and say join, or have it pick up the link from your calendar, and that it captures notes, decisions, and action items. @romanugarte_ noted you can ask the Bot questions during a meeting and even assign it action items. One limit worth knowing: in a reply from @grok, it explained that meetings only cover video calls, so in-person meetings still need a transcript or notes added by hand.

Which business meetings are a good fit?

Meetings where the follow-through matters more than the conversation itself. Good first candidates include:

  • Weekly team check-ins, where the Bot can list who owns what and remind people later.
  • Project and vendor calls, where decisions are easy to lose between emails.
  • Sales and discovery calls, where a clean summary and next steps make the follow-up faster and more accurate.
  • Internal training sessions, where the Bot can answer questions from documents it already knows.

Be more careful with calls that involve health, legal, or financial details, HR conversations, or anyone who has not agreed to an AI participant. For those, a human note-taker or no notes at all may be the better choice.

How do you set it up?

Pick one Bot for meetings, give it a link, and tell it what you want afterward. A simple starting routine:

  1. Check your plan. Meetings are on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans. On Enterprise, an admin turns Meetings on in settings.
  2. Choose the right Bot. Send the Bot that already knows the project or client, so it can answer questions with real context. A shared Team Bot works well for meetings everyone attends.
  3. Give it the link and a brief. Paste the meeting link and say what you need: for example, "join, capture decisions and owners, and send me a summary with action items when it ends."
  4. Review the first few summaries. Compare them to your own notes. If something is missed, fix the Bot's instructions or memory so the fix sticks.

What about consent and privacy?

Tell everyone the Bot is there, and get agreement before anything is recorded or transcribed. Recording and transcription rules vary by state, and some US states require consent from everyone on the call. A short line at the start of the meeting covers it: say an AI assistant is taking notes, what it will do with them, and that anyone can ask for it to leave. Also decide where summaries go. A recap that lands in a shared channel is seen by everyone in that channel, so send client call summaries only to the people who need them.

How do you turn meeting notes into follow-up that actually happens?

Connect the summary to the systems your team already works in, and keep approvals on for anything that goes to a customer. A summary is only useful if someone acts on it. With the right connections, a Bot can draft the follow-up email from a sales call, create tasks for each action item, and add notes to the contact record so the next person has the full story. Start with drafts you approve, then relax approvals only on low-risk steps that have held up.

Frequently asked questions

Which plans let Grok Bot join meetings?

According to the Grok Bot changelog, meetings are available on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans. On Enterprise, an admin turns Meetings on in settings.

Which meeting apps does Grok Bot support?

Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. You give the Bot a link to the call and it joins as a participant.

Can Grok Bot take notes at an in-person meeting?

Not directly. Meetings work on video calls. For an in-person meeting, you can record it yourself with everyone's permission and give the Bot the transcript or your notes to summarize.

Do I need to tell people the Bot is on the call?

Yes. Announce it at the start and get agreement before anything is recorded or transcribed. Some US states require consent from everyone on a call, and clients will trust you more when nothing is hidden.

Can the Bot act on what was decided in the meeting?

It can turn decisions into a summary and action items, and with the right connections it can draft follow-up emails or update records. Keep approvals on for anything that sends messages or changes customer data.
