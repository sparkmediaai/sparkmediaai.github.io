What Should You Check When Grok Bot Gets an Update?

November 10, 2026 · By David McCormick · 6 min read

When Grok Bot gets an update, run a few real test tasks, confirm your standing rules live in memory and skills instead of old chat messages, and keep approvals on for anything that sends, pays, or changes records until the results look right. Updates usually make the bot better, but any change in behavior can affect work you rely on. A short, repeatable check takes about fifteen minutes and tells you whether your setup still does what your business needs.

Why does Grok Bot's behavior change from week to week?

Because both the app and the model behind it are updated often. The official Grok Bot changelog shows multiple releases in early November 2026, covering things like refund requests, scheduled reorders with one approval each, attachments on email draft cards, and a warning when a web page hides instructions for AI agents. Separately, the Grok models themselves keep moving. On November 10, @elonmusk wrote that pre-training for Grok 4.8 is done and the model is now in reinforcement learning.

Fixes land quickly too. On November 8 @elonmusk said fixes for token efficiency and smarter behavior were underway, and the next day he posted that the team was implementing fixes overnight. Users notice these shifts. One post from @Juanmmolinag described having to remind the bot of instructions given days earlier. For a business, the lesson is simple: expect change, and have a way to check it.

Where should your business rules live so updates don't erase them?

In the bot's memory, its profile, and saved skills, not in a chat message from last Tuesday. Anything you want followed every time, such as your service area, hours, tone, prices it may quote, and who approves what, should be written down where the bot reads it on every run. A useful habit:

  • Memory for lasting facts and preferences, like "never quote a price without the current price sheet."
  • Skills for repeatable jobs, like how to write a follow-up after a quote or how to summarize new leads each morning.
  • Routines for anything that happens on a schedule, with the steps written as the outcome you want.

When you catch the bot ignoring a rule, fix the memory or skill, not just the one reply. That way the correction survives the next update.

What should be on your post-update checklist?

A handful of real tasks with known good answers, run the same way each time. Keep the list short so you actually do it. For most small businesses, five checks cover the risk:

  1. Intake. Ask it to summarize the newest inquiries from your website form or inbox. Check that it caught every one and labeled them correctly.
  2. A common reply. Have it draft an answer to a question you get every week. Compare tone and facts to a reply you already approved.
  3. A CRM lookup. Ask about a specific contact or appointment in GoHighLevel and confirm the details match the record.
  4. A follow-up list. Ask for quotes or leads that need a follow-up this week. Make sure it respects consent and opt-outs.
  5. A safety check. Confirm that sending, paying, and record changes still stop for your approval.

Save the checklist itself as a skill so anyone on the team can run it with one request.

Should you keep approvals on during an update week?

Yes, for anything customers see or anything that moves money or data. Approvals cost a few taps and protect you while you confirm the bot still behaves. If the test tasks look right for a few days, relax approvals again on the simple, low-risk work you had already trusted. If something looks off, pull that task type back to approval and fix the rule behind it.

How do you keep track of what changed?

Read the changelog once a week and keep a one-page log of your own. The changelog tells you what the app can now do. Your log records what you noticed: the date, the task, what went wrong or got better, and what you changed in memory or skills. Over a few weeks this becomes the fastest way to spot a pattern and to explain your setup to a new team member. You can even have the bot draft the weekly entry from its own notes for you to review.

Frequently asked questions

How often does Grok Bot change?

Often. The official changelog at x.ai/changelog/bot listed several releases in the first week of November 2026 alone, and the underlying Grok models are updated separately. Plan for regular change rather than a fixed version.

Why does Grok Bot sometimes forget instructions I gave it?

Instructions given once in a chat are easy to lose after updates or long conversations. Rules you want followed every time belong in the bot's memory, its profile, or a saved skill, where it reads them on every run.

Should I turn off automatic updates?

For most small businesses, no. Updates bring fixes and safety improvements. A better approach is a short test routine you run after an update, plus approvals on anything that sends, pays, or changes records.

What is a good test task for my business bot?

Pick real, repeatable work with a known right answer: summarize yesterday's new leads, draft a reply to a common question, or prepare a follow-up list from your CRM. Compare the output to what a good result looked like last week.

Who should own checking the bot after an update?

One person who knows the business rules. They run the test tasks, note anything that changed, and update the memory or skills so the fix sticks for everyone.
