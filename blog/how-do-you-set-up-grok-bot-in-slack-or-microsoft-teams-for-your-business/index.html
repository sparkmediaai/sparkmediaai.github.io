How Do You Set Up Grok Bot in Slack or Microsoft Teams for Your Business?

November 27, 2026 · By David McCormick · 7 min read

Add a shared Team Bot to Slack or Microsoft Teams, give it one clear job in one channel, and have it reply only when someone mentions it. The November 24 Grok Bot release made both apps easier to set up and better behaved: Bots now hand work to each other inside Slack, keep replies short, and in Teams can answer people who have not linked an account yet. Start small, keep anything a customer will see behind approval, and widen the Bot's reach once your team trusts it.

Why put Grok Bot in Slack or Teams instead of the app?

Because that is where your team already talks, and work there is visible to everyone in the channel. In the Grok Bot app, each person's chat with a Team Bot is private. In a Slack channel, thread or group message, or a Microsoft Teams channel, the whole team sees the request and the answer, which makes the Bot a shared teammate instead of a private helper.

SpaceXSI described this when it introduced Grok Bot 1.0: teams can share Bots and bring them into Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls. It works best for questions several people ask, such as "where is this lead in the pipeline," "what did we quote this customer," or "summarize yesterday's missed calls."

What changed in the November 24 release?

Slack Bots got better manners and better handoffs, and Microsoft Teams got simpler to connect. The Grok Bot changelog for version 1.3.0 lists, among other items:

  • When a Bot hands work to another Bot, the answer comes back in your Slack direct message, with a Connect button for anything that Bot still needs.
  • Saying a Bot's name in a Slack group direct message wakes it, the same as an @mention.
  • A thread reply can also be shown in the channel when you ask, and whoever a post was written for can ask the Bot to delete it.
  • Slack replies are one short plain paragraph unless you ask for a breakdown, and the Bot does not answer a thank-you with another message.
  • A Bot tagged in a Slack or Teams thread keeps hearing later messages instead of leaving after five silent replies.
  • Connecting Microsoft Teams no longer needs an admin to approve channel-message access first, Teams shows in the connector catalog, and someone who has not linked a Grok Bot account can still get a reply from the team's Bot.

The same release added two organization controls that matter here: members can be limited to connecting accounts on your own domains, and team admins can require approval before someone who opens an invite link joins.

How do you add a Bot to Slack?

Share the Bot with your team first, then use Add to Slack and link each person's account. The steps are short:

  1. Create the Bot as a Team Bot and share it with the people who will use it. Since November 12, the Add to Slack option shows only on Bots you have shared with others, and you can also start Slack setup from the Bot's own chat.
  2. Invite the Bot to one channel, not every channel. Pick the channel where its job lives, such as a leads channel or a support channel.
  3. Set its reply mode. Mentions only is the calm choice for a busy channel: the Bot ignores plain thread replies but still hears answers to its own questions.
  4. Have each teammate link their Grok Bot account. Someone who mentions the Bot without a linked account gets a Link Account button and a private link, and some features stay limited until they do.

Routines tied to a private Slack channel or direct message only run while the person who set them up is still a member through their linked account, so set up channel routines from an account that will stay.

How do you add a Bot to Microsoft Teams?

A team admin turns it on, an owner or Manager connects the Bot, and a Microsoft Teams admin approves it. That is how the November 11 release described the flow for Team Bots, and the November 24 release removed the separate step of approving channel-message access first. Signing in to Microsoft with a work or school account can now connect Teams along with the other Microsoft apps, such as Outlook and OneDrive.

Two details are worth knowing before you roll it out. People in Teams who have not linked a Grok Bot account can still get a reply from the team's Bot, with a one-time note about what linking adds. And asking the Bot to greet or ping someone @mentions their full name, so a Bot mentioned that way also wakes and can answer.

How do you keep several Bots in one channel from getting noisy?

Give each Bot one role, require a mention, and let them hand off instead of all answering at once. As @ThatAIStuff noted, in Slack you can mention more than one Bot in the same thread, for example a copy review Bot handing work to a launch Bot. With the November 24 change, the finished answer from a handoff lands in your direct message, so the channel does not fill up with back and forth.

A few habits help:

  • Name Bots by job. "Intake," "Follow-up" and "Reports" are easier to mention correctly than clever names.
  • Write the channel rules into each Bot. Tell it which channel it answers in, what it should never post there, and who to tag when it is unsure.
  • Keep drafts as drafts. Anything meant for a customer, such as an email or text, should come back as a draft for a person to approve. Our post on setting Grok Bot tool permissions covers how to require that.
  • Watch for reconnect cards. Since November 18, when a routine needs you to sign back in to an account, the card also arrives as a Slack or Teams direct message.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Team plan to use Grok Bot in Slack or Teams?

Sharing a Bot with coworkers and bringing it into Slack or Microsoft Teams is built around Team Bots, which are part of team plans. Check your plan in Grok Bot before you start.

Can teammates see my private chats with a Team Bot?

No. Each person's chat with a Team Bot in the Grok Bot app is private. What you say in a shared Slack or Teams channel is visible to everyone in that channel.

Will the Bot answer every message in a channel?

Not if you set Slack's reply mode to Mentions only. The Bot then answers when it is mentioned, or when someone replies to its own question.

Can someone use the Bot in Teams without a Grok Bot account?

Since November 24, someone who has not linked a Grok Bot account can still get a reply from the team's Bot in Microsoft Teams, with a one-time note on what linking adds.

Can I stop people from connecting personal accounts to a work Bot?

Yes. An organization can limit its members to connecting accounts on the organization's own domains, which keeps personal email and other accounts out of work Bots.
