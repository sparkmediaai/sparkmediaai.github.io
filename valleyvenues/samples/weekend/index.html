How the weekend lives

Friday through Sunday, the gates close behind you.

You do not visit for an afternoon. Your people arrive, settle, and stay. The rooms below are simply where those hours take place.

Friday

Arrive. Rehearse. Exhale.

Cars turn down Pope Creek Road and the week falls away. Bags find the cottages and rooms. Someone pours a first drink on the Lookout Deck while the light goes gold over the tree line.

Evening belongs to the rehearsal — often a long table in The Valley, or a quieter supper near Magnolia House. Nobody drives back to town. The estate is already theirs.

Saturday

The day you came for.

Morning is unhurried. A bride at the mirror in Magnolia House — an 1890s house that still knows how to hold a quiet hour. Vows, when you are ready, in The Valley. Later, Davis Hall takes the dinner, the toasts, the dancing.

You are not shuttling a guest list between addresses. The weekend has one address.

Sunday

Brunch, then a slower goodbye.

The lawn is still yours in the morning. Coffee, a last table, photographs you did not have time for yesterday. Guests leave when they are ready — not when a clock on a banquet contract says so.

Lodging

Sleep where the weekend happens.

About thirty-two guests can stay on the estate. That is enough for the people who should wake up here — parents, the wedding party, the ones you want at breakfast in their robes.

Everyone else is a short drive from Chattanooga. The difference is who gets to treat this place as home.

Places inside the weekend

Not a catalog. A path.

  • The Valley

    Where Saturday usually finds its voice — an open lawn and a tree line that does the decorating.

  • Davis Hall

    Dinner, dancing, the speeches that go long. A hall that can hold the night without hurrying it.

  • Lookout Deck

    Friday’s first toast. Saturday’s cocktail hour. The view you will point to in photographs later.

  • Magnolia House

    An 1890s house for getting ready, portraits, and the kind of quiet a wedding day still needs.

The only next step

Two hours on the grounds with Kobi.

Kobi Cummings will spend two hours with you — walking, sitting, asking how your people actually live. Paul, her father, keeps this place beside her. They are hosts, not a sales floor.

When you are ready, request that time. Everything else waits until you have stood here together.
